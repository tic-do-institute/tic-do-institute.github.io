About the Institute

TIC-DO Institute is an independent research organization dedicated to the computational modeling of biological systems. We investigate how information-metabolic constraints shape adaptive regulation and macroscopic physiological phenomenology through the lens of dynamical systems and complex systems theory.

Our mission is to bridge the gap between theoretical biophysics and empirical neurophysiological data. Specifically, we focus on identifying the minimal dynamical mechanisms of constrained adaptive responsiveness, isolating phenomenological frameworks from microscopic biological claims, and maintaining strict epistemic boundaries in our evaluation of biological systems.

Current Research

Manuscript Submitted to PNAS Asymptotic Compression of Adaptive Responsiveness under Information–Metabolic Constraints Principal Investigator: Takafumi Shiga

This project establishes a minimal phenomenological dynamical framework to model the trade-off between the informational benefit of precision regulation and its convex metabolic cost. We analytically demonstrate how adaptive systems undergo a continuous "Asymptotic Compression" into a Restricted Efficiency Regime (RER)—a strictly monostable dynamical regime characterized by asymptotically vanishing macroscopic constraint susceptibility, without requiring classical topological bifurcations or catastrophic regime shifts.

Crucially, this theoretical mechanism is empirically evaluated across five open neurophysiological datasets using a Standardized Evaluation Framework. The study provides partial and heterogeneous empirical observations consistent with the framework, highlighting that the macroscopic expressions of restricted dynamics (e.g., spectral steepening, temporal complexity trajectories) are context- and architecture-dependent, rather than universal.

Ongoing / Data Collection OSF Preregistered Empirical Investigation of Adaptive Recovery in Human Postural Control Principal Investigator: Takafumi Shiga (N=40) | Embargoed Pipeline (Frozen)

An independent empirical study designed to evaluate adaptive recovery and escape dynamics in macroscopic human systems. This project reframes the human axial skeleton as a high-density proprioceptive information channel and investigates how specific, high-derivative sensory-informational perturbations (SIP) influence postural dynamics.

Protocol Evolution Note: To ensure strict epistemological consistency with our biophysical paradigm, an initial exploratory clinical registry (UMIN000060274, used for pilot data acquisition N=20) was formally concluded. The definitive experimental protocol, including a deterministically frozen computational pipeline (Layer 1-4 analyses), is now strictly preregistered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) to prevent analytical bias and ensure a robust, independent empirical test.
